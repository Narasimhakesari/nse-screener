NIFTY weekly trade

Loading this week's trade

One trade a week on NIFTY. On the second trading day before each weekly expiry, at about 3:15 pm, sell the at-the-money call and put and buy a call about 4% above and a put about 4% below as protection. Hold until 3:15 pm on expiry day, then close all four. The protective buys cap the most you can lose in one week.

The four legs

How to place it in Zerodha Kite

  1. On the entry day, at about 3:15 pm, note NIFTY's level and type it into the box above. It gives the four strikes for the coming weekly expiry.
  2. Buy the two protective legs first (the far call and the far put), then sell the at-the-money call and put. Buying first gets you the lower, hedged margin. A Kite basket order can place all four together.
  3. Leave it alone until 3:15 pm on expiry day, then close all four legs. Do not add a stop-loss: the test had none, and the protective legs already cap the loss.
  4. Use 1 lot per ₹2.5 lakh or so in the account. The margin is about ₹1.5 lakh per lot, and a bad run in the test took about ₹90,000 off a lot before recovering.
  5. If an NSE holiday falls in the week, the entry day moves one trading day earlier. This page counts weekdays only, so check the exchange holiday list in holiday weeks.

How it did

1 lot, after costs, before tax

Before you trade it

  • About 4 weeks in 10 lose money. The worst single week in the test lost about ₹37,000 per lot, and losing runs lasted months.
  • The protective buys cost about half the profit in the test, because NIFTY rarely moved 4% in two days. They are insurance against a crash bigger than anything from 2019 to 2026; without them that loss would be unlimited.
  • The test covers 2019–2026 only, about 400 weekly trades. Weekly expiry days, lot sizes and margin rules have changed several times, and may change again.
  • Prices here are NSE closing prices, which differ a little from 3:15 pm prices, and far strikes can be thin, so your fills may be worse.
  • Tax: F&O profits are business income, taxed at your slab rate. SEBI found about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lose money.

Not investment advice. A rules-based example from public NSE data, for education. Not a recommendation and not research by a SEBI-registered analyst. Options can lose more than you expect.
