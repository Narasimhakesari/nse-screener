NSE Stock Agent

Buy strong NSE stocks. Sell at +20% or −10%.

Each evening the agent finds stocks that have just joined the 30 strongest of the 300 most-traded NSE stocks. You buy at the next open, then place one GTT order in Zerodha with a 20% target and a 10% stop, so your broker sells for you the moment either price is hit. Anything still open after 21 trading days, you sell. At most 5 trades at a time.

The market is cautious. Fewer stocks are in uptrends. In the test, skipping new buys at times like this did worse ( a year, worst fall ) than buying anyway (, worst fall ). The rule keeps buying; smaller amounts are the calmer choice.

      How to use it

      1. 1

        Check tonight's buys

        The list updates after 8:30 pm IST on trading days; with alerts on, you get it by message. Put one fifth of your trading money into each new buy. If all five slots are full, there is nothing to buy.

      2. 2

        Buy at the open

        Next morning, buy at the open. In Kite you can place it the night before as an AMO (after-market order) at market price, which fills at the open.

      3. 3

        Place one GTT OCO straight away

        In Kite, open the stock in Holdings, choose Create GTT, then OCO (One Cancels Other). Stop-loss trigger: your buy price −10%, as a market order with protection so it fills. Target trigger: your buy price +20%, as a limit order at that price. Type your buy price into the trade's card here and it gives you both numbers. When one triggers, Zerodha cancels the other.

      4. 4

        Sell anything left on its sell-by date

        If neither price is hit within 21 trading days, sell at the open on the sell-by date shown on the card (an AMO the evening before works), then delete its GTT. About 6 in 10 trades end this way.

      5. 5

        Take every signal, including the losers

        About half the trades lose money and the worst fall in the test was over a third. The rule works on average across many trades; skipping some or moving the stop breaks it. Only use money you can afford to lose.
